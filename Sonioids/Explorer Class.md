Small bat-like salamanders with claws used for digging through soft ground and picking up objects.

Explorer class sonioids have wings similar to that of a bat, with webbing connecting the sides of their torsos to their front limbs, these wings allow them to glide in most atmospheres, and fly in some of the more dense ones, although they are prone to tearing if the atmosphere is too dense or if the wind is too heavy.

Explorer class sonioids also have webbing between their fingers and toes, an artifact from their original deployment as aquatic robots used to scan the Mariana Trench on Earth.

Explorer class sonioids can retract their claws to avoid scratching/damaging delicate materials, such as gemstones or fossils.

Explorer class sonioids have a built-in mapping processor intended to scan and store the terrain to an incredibly high resolution, this processor, and its storage, is located near the base of their neck, in between their shoulder blades. This storage is also used to mark threats and points of interest which can be referenced later as the sonioid is retracing their route.

Explorer class sonioids largely travel alone, but can rarely be seen with another explorer class sonioid or a research class sonioid. If they are with a research class sonioid, they are likely tethered to the sonioid to prevent them from straying to far and leaving the research class sonioid vulnerable to attack. 

Explorer class sonioids have a limited ability to swim, they instead use their claws and webbing to climb underwater terrain and float between large gaps if necessary, more often than not, they rely on their research class sonioid to help them return to the nest in underwater environments.
